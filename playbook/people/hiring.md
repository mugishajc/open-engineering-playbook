# Hiring

"We cannot hire senior engineers, and the ones we hire do not always work out." Run every engineering hire through a structured process: a written role profile, the same questions for every candidate, a realistic work sample, and independent scoring against a scorecard before anyone discusses the candidate. Research on selection methods finds that structured interviews and work samples predict job performance better than unstructured conversations. A structured process is also faster, fairer and easier to defend.

## Outcomes

Starting points. Tune them to your organisation.

- Time from application to offer: 21 calendar days or less for 80% of hires.
- 100% of interviewers have completed interview training and score independently before the debrief.
- Every candidate receives a decision within 5 working days of their last interview.
- 90-day retention of new hires at 95% or above, and hiring manager satisfaction at 90 days of at least 4 out of 5.
- The share of candidates who withdraw after an interview is tracked, with reasons collected.

## The process

| Step | What happens | Time box (starting point) |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Role profile | Hiring manager writes the outcomes for the first year, the skills needed, and the level from the [career framework](career-framework.md). Must-haves are few and real. | Before the job is posted |
| 2. Job advert | Plain language, salary range stated where law and policy allow, must-haves separated from nice-to-haves | 1 day |
| 3. Screen | 30-minute call: motivation, logistics, one or two technical questions from a fixed list | Within 5 working days of application |
| 4. Work sample | A task close to the real job (see below) | 2 hours maximum for a take-home, or a 60 to 90 minute live session |
| 5. Structured interviews | 2 or 3 interviews, each assessing named competencies with fixed questions and a scoring guide | All within 1 week |
| 6. Independent scoring | Each interviewer completes the [scorecard](../../templates/people/interview-scorecard.md) before seeing other scores | Within 24 hours of the interview |
| 7. Debrief and decision | Hiring manager decides, using the scorecards and evidence, not general impressions | Within 2 working days of the last interview |
| 8. Offer | Verbal then written offer | Within 2 working days of the decision |

## Work samples

- Base the task on real work: review a pull request with planted problems, extend a small service, debug a failing test, design a system on a whiteboard with real constraints, write an incident update.
- Give every candidate the same task and the same scoring guide.
- Respect people's time. A take-home task should take no more than 2 hours. Pay candidates for longer exercises or offer a live alternative. Long unpaid tasks screen out people with caring duties and second jobs.
- Discuss the work sample in the next interview. How the candidate explains choices and trade-offs often tells you more than the code.

## Candidates and code assistants

Engineers use code assistants at work (see [code assistants](code-assistants.md)). Decide your rule for interviews, tell candidates in advance, and apply it the same way to everyone.

| Approach | How it works |
|---|---|
| Tools allowed | The candidate may use approved assistants. Score how they frame the problem, verify output, test, and explain every line. This matches the job. |
| Tools-free segment | A short part of the interview without assistants to check fundamentals: reading code, debugging, reasoning about complexity and failure. |
| Live explanation | Whatever the tools, the candidate walks through the code and answers "why this, not that?" and "how would this fail in production?" |

A blend of all three works well. Never assume a candidate cheated because their code is good: ask them to explain it.

## Structured, inclusive and skills-based

- Write questions and scoring guides in advance for each competency. Use behavioural questions ("tell me about a time...") and situational questions ("what would you do if...").
- Score on a fixed scale with written anchors (for example 1 to 4, with an example of each level).
- Use diverse interview panels where you can, and train every interviewer, including on bias in assessment.
- Remove requirements that are proxies rather than skills: specific degrees, years of experience with one tool, or a particular employer. Test the skill directly.
- Offer reasonable adjustments to every candidate before the interview: extra time, a different format, captions, breaks.
- Hire for learning ability as well as current skills. The stack will change. See [skills gap](skills-gap.md).

## Practice

### Foundation

- A written role profile and a fixed set of questions. One short, realistic work sample.
- Two people interview. They score separately before they talk.
- Decide within a week of the last interview.

### Standard

- Scorecards for every role family, using the [interview scorecard template](../../templates/people/interview-scorecard.md), linked to the career framework.
- Interviewer training is required before anyone joins a panel. Interviewers shadow 2 interviews and are reverse-shadowed once.
- Hiring metrics per role: time to offer, candidate withdrawal rate, offer acceptance rate, 90-day retention. Reviewed quarterly.
- The interview process and its questions are reviewed every 12 months, or sooner if outcomes slip.

### Assured

- Hiring decisions are documented with scorecards and evidence, retained for the period employment law and your policy require, and handled as personal data under the law that applies (for example EU GDPR, the Kenya Data Protection Act 2019 or Brazil's LGPD).
- Pre-employment screening and vetting required for the role (for example security clearance) are scheduled early, because they often take the longest.
- If you use AI tools to screen or rank candidates, treat them as high risk. The EU AI Act lists AI used in recruitment and worker management as high-risk (Annex III), with obligations applying from 2 Dec 2027. Check EUR-Lex for current dates, and see [AI governance](../ai/ai-governance.md).
- Public sector: follow the civil service or public body recruitment principles that apply, including fair and open competition.

## Cloud, on-premises and air-gapped

Roles on air-gapped or classified systems often need security clearance before the person can start the real work. Start vetting at offer, plan unclassified onboarding work for the waiting period (see [onboarding](onboarding.md)), and say the expected timeline in the job advert.

## Anti-patterns

| Anti-pattern | Fix |
|---|---|
| "Culture fit" as a reason to reject | Assess named values with structured questions. Record evidence. |
| Each interviewer asks whatever they like | Fixed questions and scoring guides per competency. |
| Group debrief before scores are written | Independent scorecards first. |
| Puzzle questions unrelated to the job | Work samples based on real tasks. |
| Six or more interview rounds over a month | 3 to 4 steps after screening, all within 1 week. |
| Banning code assistants without telling candidates | Decide the rule, publish it in advance, assess explanation either way. |
