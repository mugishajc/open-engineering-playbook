## How to use the answers

A good answer has a number, a trend over at least 4 quarters and a named owner. A worrying answer is vague, has no date, or blames one person or supplier. A worrying answer is a prompt to ask for a plan, not a verdict. Two worrying answers on the same question in a row is a matter for the board, not only the meeting.

## The ten questions

### 1. Which three business outcomes is technology working on this quarter, and how will we know they moved?

- Good answer: three outcomes from the company scorecard, each with a measure, a baseline and a target date.
- Worrying answer: a list of projects or technologies with no business measure.
- Answer: [...]

### 2. How long does it take for a small, approved change to reach customers, and is that getting shorter?

- Good answer: change lead time in hours or days, with a 4-quarter trend.
- Worrying answer: weeks or months, or "it depends on the release window".
- Answer: [...]

### 3. How often do changes cause problems for customers, and how fast do we recover?

- Good answer: change fail rate and failed deployment recovery time, stable or improving while speed improves.
- Worrying answer: not measured, or failures recovered in days.
- Answer: [...]

### 4. Which services matter most to customers, what reliability have we promised, and are we meeting it?

- Good answer: a short list of services with service level objectives agreed with the business, and attainment for each.
- Worrying answer: "everything is critical" or "we aim for 100%".
- Answer: [...]

### 5. When did we last restore our most important systems from backup, and how long did it take?

- Good answer: for Tier 1 systems, an automated restore test every month and a full restore exercise in the last quarter, with the measured time compared with the restore recovery time objective. See [backup and recovery](../../playbook/reliability/backup-and-recovery.md).
- Worrying answer: "the backups run every night" with no restore test.
- Answer: [...]

### 6. What are our top five technology risks, and who owns each?

- Good answer: five risks from the risk register, each with an owner, a rating, the next action and a date.
- Worrying answer: generic risks ("cyber attack") with no owner or action.
- Answer: [...]

### 7. Which suppliers could stop the business if they failed, and could we leave them?

- Good answer: named critical suppliers, the services they support, and an exit plan with cost and time, tested in the last year.
- Worrying answer: no list, or exit "would be discussed at renewal".
- Answer: [...]

### 8. What share of engineering time goes to running and fixing existing systems, and is it rising?

- Good answer: a measured split (run, grow, transform, or features, reliability, debt and platform) with a trend.
- Worrying answer: unknown, or rising every quarter with no plan.
- Answer: [...]

### 9. Where are we using AI, with what data, and who approved it?

- Good answer: an AI register with each use, its risk tier, the data it uses and the approver.
- Worrying answer: "teams are experimenting" with no register or policy.
- Answer: [...]

### 10. Which people could we not afford to lose, and what are we doing about it?

- Good answer: named key-person risks, a skills matrix, and actions such as pairing, documentation and hiring.
- Worrying answer: "the team is stable" or no answer.
- Answer: [...]
